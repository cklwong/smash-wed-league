    FAQ

    How Smash Wed works — the weekly singles league and the monthly doubles (team relay) night.

    Singles league every Wednesday

    How do I sign up?

    Use the Join tab and pick a Wednesday. Signups close at noon Pacific on match day. The first 24 players are confirmed; everyone after that is on the waitlist and moves up automatically if a confirmed player drops. Please bring 2 good quality birds; court rental is $5/person.

    How are pools drawn?

    At check-in the organizer draws round-robin pools by standings — the top-ranked players go to Pool A, the next group to Pool B, and so on (2 pools for up to 16 players, 3 for up to 21, 4 above that). New players without a ranking are placed last. If someone doesn't show, a Guest takes their spot so the pool stays even.

    How are games scored and how do I earn ranking points?

    You play everyone in your pool once. Your rank points for the night are the number of games you won, plus a +1 bonus for playing in a top pool (Pool A when there are 3 pools; Pools A and B when there are 4).

    How are the standings calculated?

    Your average is the mean of your best 4 weeks out of your last 6 weeks played. Weeks you skip don't count against you — but a 4th missed week in a row is marked "1mo absence" with 0 points until you've played 2 weeks again.

    Players tied on average are separated by rank points in the most recent week they all played, then by their most recent head-to-head result. Anyone still tied shares the rank.

    Doubles (team relay) last Wednesday of the month

    What is it?

    Once a month the singles league takes a break for a team night. Everyone who signs up is split into 4 or 6 teams (up to 36 players), and teams play doubles relays against each other. Sign up on the Join tab the same way — doubles (team relay) nights are labelled there.

    How are teams formed?

    Teams are drawn from the standings "snake" style (1st pick to Team A, 2nd to B, … then back the other way), so every team gets a similar mix. Teams are split as evenly as possible, so a team can have fewer than 6 players. The organizer can move players between teams and add late arrivals or guests. Each team picks a captain.

    Who plays with whom?

    The captain puts the team in order P1, P2, P3 … and the doubles pairs rotate: P1+P2, P2+P3, P3+P4, P4+P5, P5+P6 and P6+P1. With 6 players that's 6 pairs, and everyone plays in exactly 2 of them each round. The captain also decides the order the pairs play in.

    Between rounds the captain can change the pairings for round 2 by putting the team in a new P1, P2, P3 … order (and a new playing order) before round 2's first game.

    How does a match between two teams work?
    • Team A plays Team B, Team C plays Team D, and so on. Each team's first pair plays the other team's first pair, then the second pairs, etc.
    • Each doubles game is to 15 points (win by 2, capped at 21) unless the organizer announces a different target that night.
    • A round is won by the team that wins more doubles games; if that's level, by total point difference.
    • Teams play two rounds. If each team wins one, one tiebreak doubles game to 15 decides the match — each captain picks any two players for it.
    • Both teams in a match need the same number of players — the organizer evens it out with a guest or late player if needed.
    Does it count for the standings?

    The organizer decides for each relay night. An exhibition night doesn't affect anyone's standings at all — it's as if the week didn't happen. On a ranked night you earn one point per doubles game you win (up to 4 over the two rounds) plus 2 bonus points if your team wins its match — at most 6, the same scale as a singles night. The tiebreak game only decides the match; guests don't earn points.
